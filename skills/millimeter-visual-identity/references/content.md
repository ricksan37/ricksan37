## Content fundamentals

**Voice.** Direct, confident, warm, never corporate-grey. Short sentences. One idea per line. A
little dry humour is welcome. Filler is not. The system is French-first (the brand source and the
sample slides are French), and the same rules apply in English.

**The dash rule, non-negotiable.** No dash anywhere, in prose or in nomenclature. Never use an em
dash or a hyphen as a rhetorical break inside a sentence: rewrite with a full stop, a comma, a colon
or parentheses. Kickers do not carry one either.

- Wrong: "It is more than a place to stay, it is where you belong."
- Right: "It is more than a place to stay. It is where you belong."
- Wrong: "Fully furnished, move-in ready, designed for ease."
- Right: "Fully furnished and move-in ready, designed for ease."

The one surviving exception is the numeric range, where the en dash is a mathematical operator and
not punctuation: `12–20px`, `15–18pt`.

**Casing.** Sentence case for headlines and body. UPPERCASE only for mono labels, kickers, table
headers, tags and axis labels, always letter-spaced. Never uppercase a headline.

**Person.** Plain declaratives about the work, not about the reader. "Stream ingest passed batch for
the first time", not "You'll love what happened to your ingest". "We" appears when a team owns an
action ("Tout ce que nous lisons"). "You" is rare and only in instructions.

**Numbers.** Always in mono. Thousands separated by a thin space in French copy (`1 284 902`).
Deltas carry their sign (`+4.2 pts`, `-0.06s`). Ranges use an en dash (`12–20px`).

**Kickers.** Every content slide, card group and document section opens with one: a two-digit index,
a space, then one or two words. `01 PALETTE`. `DATA PRINCIPLES`. `SOURCES`.

**Emoji: never.** Not in slides, not in dashboards, not in documents, not in labels. Icons are line
glyphs from the icon set. Unicode is used for exactly two marks: the trailing arrow `→` on a primary
button, and the middle dot `·` as a separator in mono strings.

**Vibe check before shipping copy.** Read it aloud. If a sentence contains a dash acting as a
pause, rewrite it. If a sentence could appear in any SaaS deck, cut it.
